1.0 What this sheet answers

Your cell is discontinued. This is what fits, and what the difference does.

Two hundred seventy-seven of the 416 cells in this catalog are out of production. A cell that drops into the compartment is not automatically a safe stand-in: the same 11.6 × 5.4 mm shell was built as an alkaline cell at 1.50 V, a silver-oxide cell at 1.55 V, and a mercuric-oxide cell at 1.35 V. An instrument calibrated for the mercury cell will read wrong on the alkaline one, quietly, for years.

Fits

Same shell. Diameter and height match to the tenth of a millimeter, so the contact seats and the lid closes. Nothing about the reading is promised.

Fits and reads wrong

Same shell, different chemistry. It powers the device and shifts every value the device reports. On this sheet that condition is orange, everywhere, and it is the only thing that is.

Steel bar: within 50 mV, swap it. Orange bar: it fits and the reading moves.

The rail is zero-suppressed on purpose. On an absolute 0–3 V scale the difference between 1.35 and 1.55 V is a hair; on the instrument it is the difference between a correct exposure and two thirds of a stop.

6.0 Discontinued 277 items · two thirds of the catalog

A discontinued cell is drawn hollow: outline only, dashed, nothing inside it, because there is nothing to buy under that code. Its row carries a broken rail instead of a solid one, and it is never left without a destination — every dead code points at the cell that takes its place, with the voltage change stated on the same line.

7.0 Method

Every dimension here is the shell figure: diameter across the can, height with the cap seated. Drawings are generated from those two numbers at a stated scale and are never resized to fit a column — if a strip is too wide for the screen, the strip scrolls and the millimeters stay honest.

Voltages are nominal open-circuit values for the chemistry, not measurements of a particular cell. Capacity is a typical rating at room temperature under a light continuous load; it is the least transferable number on the sheet, which is why it is printed last.

Where a designation was retired rather than the cell — 303 folded into SR44 — the retired code keeps its own entry, so a search for the code stamped on the metal still lands somewhere.

Advertising occupies three fixed positions: a banner above the catalog, one rectangle inside the article column, one tall unit in the reference rail. Each is drawn here filled, at its exact size. When no creative is available the element is not rendered at all — an empty box left in the flow would still count as a row and would still take a margin.
