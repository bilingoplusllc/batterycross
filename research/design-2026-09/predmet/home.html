How to read a cell here

Four marks carry everything. Learn them once and the rest of the site is a picture, not a paragraph.

Lit — in production

Solid metal, one highlight from the upper left, a shadow on the bench. You can buy it today.

Hollow — discontinued

No metal, no highlight, no shadow: the light does not land on it and it does not sit on the bench. The gasket line is broken.

The socket

Two walls and a dashed lid line, drawn from the device the cell is named for. Flush with all three means it fits. Daylight under the lid is marked.

The clash

Where the cell crosses a wall of the socket, the wall turns red and the overhang is bracketed. Nothing about that is negotiable.

03

Fits, and fits electrically

A socket built for 1.50 V physically accepts every cell in that shell. What changes is how far the cell sits from the voltage the device was calibrated against. The bar below is that distance, drawn from zero, in volts. The last row is a different shell entirely; it is here because it is the code people bring most often.

The near miss is the dangerous one. Silver oxide sits 0.05 V above alkaline and holds that voltage almost flat until it dies; alkaline sags from the first hour. In a light meter or a film camera calibrated for the 1.35 V mercury cell, the 1.50 V alkaline that drops straight into the same socket reads about two thirds of a stop off when new, and drifts the whole time it is in there.

Fitting is a fact about millimeters. Working is a fact about your device. This site measures the first one and describes the second; it does not certify a substitution. Check the manual before you trust a reading.

05

Gone, and what took the socket

Two thirds of the catalog is out of production, most of it killed by the mercury bans of the 1990s. A replacement is only a replacement if both drawings agree.

Voltage matched

Zinc-air holds 1.35 V flat, which is the property the meter was calibrated against. It breathes: once the seal comes off, it runs down in months whether you use it or not.

Fits, reads high

The drop-in everyone reaches for, and the reason this site exists. Identical can, identical socket, 0.15 V too high on day one and a different number every week after that.

Drop-in

The easy case: same shell, same chemistry, same nominal voltage, still made. Both drawings agree, so there is nothing further to say.

Method

DrawingsEvery silhouette is generated from the diameter and height in the record, at a scale printed beside it. No drawing is adjusted for appearance.
SocketsThe socket outline is the bore and depth of the cell the page is named for, not a manufacturer tolerance. Clearance is not drawn.
VoltageNominal voltage as published. Deviation is measured against the socket's own nominal, from zero, on a scale that is broken where it says it is broken.
StatusA cell counts as out of production when no listed manufacturer catalogs it. 277 of 416 records currently qualify.
Not includedPrices, sellers, and shelf life. This is a dimensional and electrical cross-reference.
